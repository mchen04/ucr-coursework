# BUS 146 — Business Model Canvas: Target Corporation

**Assignment:** Business Model Canvas analysis for a Fortune 100 company. Dr. Attaway.

**Company:** Target Corporation

## Key Findings

### Competitive Advantage (Core Moat)
**Store-as-fulfillment-center model** — ~1,950 suburban stores serve simultaneously as retail locations and same-day fulfillment hubs. 97% of digital orders fulfilled from stores, not warehouses. Structural cost advantage that compounds as digital sales grow; near-impossible for pure e-commerce players to replicate.

### Value Proposition
"Cheap chic" positioning: design-forward merchandise cheaper than specialty retail but more aspirational than Walmart. Exclusive owned brands (Cat & Jack $2B+, Good & Gather) drive higher margins and customer loyalty. RedCard (5% discount) incentivizes consolidation of household spending.

### Revenue Diversity
- **Core:** In-store + digital merchandise sales (~$110B annually)
- **Growing:** Roundel retail media network ($1.5B+, double-digit growth); first-party shopper data increasingly scarce as third-party cookies disappear
- **Financial:** RedCard interest; avoided Visa/Mastercard interchange fees (saves hundreds of millions annually)
- **Adjacent:** Shipt same-day delivery; shop-in-shop lease revenue (Ulta, Apple, Starbucks)

### Key Resources
- **Brand:** Emotionally embedded "cheap chic" identity + "Target run" cultural phenomenon (decades to build, impossible to engineer)
- **Real estate:** Suburban store density + inventory visibility tech for omnichannel fulfillment
- **Owned brands:** Exclusive design equity; competitors can build private labels but brand takes years
- **Roundel data:** First-party purchase history on 100M+ loyalty members; becomes more defensible as regulatory privacy tightens

### Key Processes
- Merchandising forecasting 12–18 months ahead
- Owned brand design/development in-house
- Store operations as fulfillment: pick/pack/curbside workflows
- Target Circle loyalty (100M+ members) + personalization
- Roundel ad campaign sales + measurement

## Scenario Component (Step 3)
Framework provided for adaptation when scenario revealed. Key risks: brand dilution if moving too far upmarket/downmarket, execution complexity for new domains (healthcare, financial services), regulatory friction on data/privacy.
